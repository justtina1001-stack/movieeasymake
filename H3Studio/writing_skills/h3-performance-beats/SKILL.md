# H3 Performance Beats — Studio adaptation

## Writing rules
Choose each shot's narrative or emotional purpose before selecting framing and camera position. Give a principal beat one readable expression or physical state change; show the trigger, gaze or breathing response, posture change and natural settling. Do not stack competing eyebrow, mouth, tear, head and hand changes at one instant. Divide overloaded material into motivated internal cuts while keeping valid 5-15 second generation clips and the supplied total duration. Beat counts are planning heuristics, not hard model limits. Silent characters stay silent: never invent a <d> line, voiceover or filler dialogue to activate facial animation. When dialogue exists, preserve its exact text and language, keep the same speaker ID and place delivery direction outside <d>. Use body performance when it tells the emotion more clearly. Budget understanding time and follow-through within the original timeline rather than adding a fixed tail or forcing a trim. Check visible cast, screen direction, cut timing and final state; do not promise facial accuracy from small-sample findings.
